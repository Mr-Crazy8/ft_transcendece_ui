The hardest part of distributed systems isn’t the distribution.

MC
Maya Chen·Distributed Systems

Verified via AI interview

It’s deciding what you can afford to get wrong. After a decade of building systems at scale, here are the trade-offs I wish we talked about more.

Start with the cost of being wrong

Every architecture encodes a set of priorities. A payment system cannot treat a duplicate charge the way an analytics dashboard treats a delayed event. Before choosing a database or a queue, write down which mistakes your users can tolerate and which ones they cannot.

Make the trade-off explicit

In one system I worked on, we traded immediate consistency for a simpler recovery path. The decision worked because the product made pending states visible and the team rehearsed failure recovery. The architecture alone was not the solution; the operational habits were part of it.

The useful question is not whether your system is distributed. It is whether your team can explain what happens when one part stops answering. Start there, test the answer, and let the design follow.
